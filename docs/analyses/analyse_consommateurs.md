# Student Deal Radar: consumer evidence and recommendations
*Desk research, 28 Oct 2026. Evidence grades: **[S]** large survey or regulator, **[V]** vendor or industry data, **[G]** gap (no solid source found).*

## 1. Price sensitivity and budgets
- **Young Europeans trade down heavily.** 80–95% of Gen Z and millennials in FR/DE/IT/ES/UK reported trade-down behaviour in late 2024, against 52–81% of older cohorts. This includes cheaper retailers, private label and extra trips to chase discounts (McKinsey ConsumerWise, n≈5,057) [S]. The 2025 updates say Gen Z still trades down the most [S].
  https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/week-in-charts/gen-z-leads-the-trade-down-trend · https://www.mckinsey.de/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/an-update-on-european-consumer-sentiment-2025
- **Cost of living is the top worry:** 52% of Gen Z live paycheck to paycheck (Deloitte Gen Z/Millennial Survey 2025, 23k respondents, 44 countries) [S].
  https://www.deloitte.com/nl/en/about/press-room/deloitte-genz-and-millennial-survey.html
- **Budget structure:** students who live away from home spend about 23% of their budget on food and about 37% on housing (EUROSTUDENT VIII). Costs rose faster than inflation in 88% of countries [S].
  https://www.eurostudent.eu/download_files/documents/Eurostudent8_2024_240618_Chapter_B8_bf.pdf
- **Switzerland:** university reference budgets put students living away from home at about CHF 1,600–2,300 a month, including about **CHF 450 for food** (ZHdK 2024/26, Uni Basel 2026, Uni Fribourg) [S, institutional].
  https://www.zhdk.ch/file/live/0b/0b93829f5b180978becfc7e56bfa496b784678e2/mb_studien_und_lebenskosten_de_2026.pdf · https://www.unibas.ch/dam/jcr:f5650a4d-6e2e-4c06-bf98-aa8128ef5d11/FRM_gh_Lebenskostenbudget_aktuelle_Version_D_20260101.2026-01-21-10-58-14.pdf
- **[G]** I found no reliable per-country split of student spending on tech or leisure. The country tables are in the EUROSTUDENT VIII synopsis (DOI 10.3278/6001920ew008).

**Implication:** groceries are the largest flexible budget line that recurs every week. Saving CHF 20–40 a month (5–9% of food spend) is a credible promise. Tech deals are rare but high-ticket, so they carry the affiliate revenue.

## 2. How deals are discovered today
- **Social media leads discovery:** 68% of Gen Z found products on social media in 2024 (60% in 2023) [V]. Among US 18–27-year-olds, the top discovery channels are Instagram (30%), TikTok (23%), Google (19%) and YouTube (15%) (GRIN) [V, US].
  https://www.entrepreneur.com/growing-a-business/6-social-media-trends-defining-gen-zs-shopping-behavior/493081 · https://searchengineland.com/google-tiktok-instagram-gen-z-shoppers-451625
- **Swiss youth apps:** the "Big Four" are Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp and Snapchat (JAMES 2024, ZHAW/Swisscom, about 1,000 people aged 12–19) [S].
  https://zhaw.ch/storage/hochschule/medien/news/2024/241128_MM_JAMES-Studie/medienmitteilung_JAMES_Studie-2024.pdf
- **Flyer habit in Switzerland:** weekly supermarket promotions matter more in Switzerland than in neighbouring countries. Migros and Coop papers each reach more than 3M readers (Blick). Profital (Swiss Post / Bring! Labs) claims about 800k downloads and 4M monthly contacts [V, self-reported].
  https://www.blick.ch/wirtschaft/ziehen-migros-und-co-nach-das-musst-du-jetzt-wissen-coop-aendert-das-regime-fuer-die-aktionstage-im-supermarkt-id20493266.html · https://business.profital.ch/en/about-us
- **Community deal sites:** Pepper (mydealz, Dealabs, hotukdeals…) reaches about 25M users a month on an affiliate model with community voting ("temperature"). Revenue is about USD 75M [V].
  https://app.dealroom.co/companies/pepper_nl
- **[G]** I found no published usage shares for Telegram or WhatsApp deal channels in Europe. One unsourced roundup says 16% of Telegram users follow discount channels.

**Implication:** students do not search for deals; deals reach them through feeds and chats. Distribution has to happen where they already are (Instagram/TikTok content, WhatsApp channel, class group chats), with the app or site as the reference layer.

## 3. Willingness to pay (premium alerts)
- RevenueCat 2026 (115k apps) gives a **median download-to-paid rate by day 35 of 2.1% for freemium apps** and 10.7% for hard paywalls. 82% of trial starts happen on install day [V, large dataset].
  https://revenuecat.com/report · https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025
- Low-priced apps get more downloads but convert worse (4.3% vs 9.8% download-to-trial for high-priced apps, 2025) [V].
- **[G]** I found no study on student willingness to pay for deal alerts specifically. Given the budget pressure in §1, I would plan for freemium conversion of **1–3% of MAU**, not 5%.

## 4. Retention benchmarks and habit drivers
- **Shopping apps:** D1 about 19–25%, D7 about 10–13%, D30 about 4–7%. AppsFlyer via Braze puts D30 at 4.6% on iOS and 4.0% on Android [V].
  https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/mobile-app-retention
- **Push notifications:** users who received at least one push in their first 90 days retained about 3× better. Weekly push gave 2–5× better retention in retail, and 95% of opted-in users who got no push churned within 90 days (Airship, 63M users; correlational) [V].
  https://www.airship.com/newsroom/urban-airships-mobile-app-retention-study-for-key-industry-verticals/
- **Habit anchor:** the Swiss flyer cycle runs weekly (promotions Tuesday to Monday), a natural trigger for a weekly digest.
- **Too Good To Go** grew mainly through word of mouth to 74M registered users in 17 countries. Its stated goal is to become a habitual first choice, not a "plan B" [V].
  https://www.marketingweek.com/too-good-to-go-plan-b-top-mind/

## 5. Virality and referral
- Typical consumer referral-program k-factor is **0.1–0.5**; inherent product sharing can reach 0.5–2 [V]. A Branch figure via GrowSurf gives 12–20% share-to-install conversion [V].
  https://growsurf.com/statistics/referral-program-benchmarks/ · https://www.branch.io/resources/blog/mobile-sharing-and-referral-feature-benchmarks-from-branch/
- Campus tactics that have worked: Too Good To Go live on campus with refectory offers (Leeds), and students recruiting merchants (U. Minnesota) [V].
  https://mndaily.com/city/too-good-to-go-tries-to-make-leftovers-affordable/04/02/2024/snoadmin/
- **[G]** I found no public figures on UNiDAYS or Student Beans ambassador performance.

## 6. Trust and honest scoring
- **EU sweep (March 2026, 314 traders):** 30% referenced discounts incorrectly (the reference must be the lowest price of the last 30 days). Of the 34% that showed price comparisons, 6 in 10 did not explain the reference price [S, regulator].
  https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-check-reveals-misleading-sales-practices-online-2026-03-26_en
- **Which?:** only 1 in 7 Black Friday deals was genuine in 2022 (86% were as cheap or cheaper in the previous six months) [S, consumer org].
  https://www.itv.com/news/2022-11-22/just-one-in-seven-black-friday-deals-offer-genuine-discount-which-finds
- Pepper's scale shows that community-verified "heat" scoring is a model people will use at scale [V].
- **[G]** I found no survey that directly measures how much consumers value transparent scoring. The case rests on how common fake discounts are.

## Prioritised recommendations
1. **(P0) Make "true discount" scoring the core feature.** Score each deal against the 30-day low and the 6-month median price, and show the price history plus "why this score". Label sponsored deals separately and never let them change the score. This is the differentiator against Profital, flyers and influencers, and it is EU-law aligned.
2. **(P0) Build distribution before the app.** Run a WhatsApp channel and an Instagram/TikTok account ("Top 5 real deals this week, St. Gallen"), with a link to the web radar. The web/PWA comes first and the native app later.
3. **(P0) Make the weekly ritual the retention engine.** Send a Monday/Tuesday "Aktionen digest" (Migros, Coop, Denner, Lidl, Aldi) with an estimated CHF saved for a typical student basket. Add opt-in push only for user-chosen categories. Ask for push permission after the first saved deal, not on first launch.
4. **(P1) Make sharing part of the product.** Use one-tap "send to flat/class chat" cards and invite links that unlock a perk (e.g. one week of instant alerts). Run 3–5 HSG ambassadors and student-association (Vereine) partnerships, plus events and freebies listings, which are inherently shareable.
5. **(P1) Monetisation order:** affiliate on tech and travel first, then local sponsors (restaurants and bars near campus, as clearly labelled slots), then premium (instant alerts, price-drop watchlist, early freebies) at about CHF 3–4/month or CHF 24/year. Offer a 14-day trial at onboarding, since most trials start on install day. Run display ads last; they dilute trust.
6. **(P2) Gamification:** show a "CHF saved this semester" counter and community votes on deal quality. Avoid streak pressure.

## Target metrics (first 6 months, St. Gallen)
| Metric | Floor (benchmark) | Target |
|---|---|---|
| D1 / D7 / D30 retention (app/PWA) | 25% / 11% / 5% | 35% / 20% / 12% (weekly ritual) |
| Weekly active / MAU | none | ≥40% |
| Push opt-in | none | ≥50% of new users |
| Free-to-paid (MAU) | 1–2% | 3% |
| k-factor | 0.1 | 0.3 |
| Share of signups via referral or chats | none | ≥30% |
| Reach | none | 2,000 of about 9,000 HSG students in semester 1 (~20%) |
| Score trust (in-app "was this score fair?") | none | ≥80% yes |

*Caveats: retention and push figures are vendor data and correlational. Most social-discovery data is US-based. For Swiss validation, a 5-question survey of 100 HSG students on spending, channels and willingness to pay would be cheap and decisive.*
