## 2. Saturated vs underserved (for student + local-city deals)

- **Saturated:** **UK** (hotukdeals, UNiDAYS/Student Beans home market, TopCashback, TGTG), **France** (Dealabs, MeinProspekt/Bonial, iGraal/Poulpeo/Rakuten, cashback culture), **Germany** (mydealz, kaufDA, marktguru, Shoop, UniNow/Studo). Each of the three verticals has a strong national leader, so a newcomer only wins at hyper-local level.
- **Moderately served:** **Nordics** (Bonial/Tjek consolidating flyers as of Oct 2026), **NL and Austria** (Pepper NL, preisjaeger, marktguru AT), **Italy and Spain** (ShopFully/Tiendeo flyers, Pepper IT/ES).
- **Underserved:** **Switzerland.** There is no Pepper site, no Bonial, and student brands are thin. Groceries split between Profital (Post), Rappn (new) and aktionis (small). Deal communities come down to Preispirat alone. Prices are high, which raises how much a deal is worth. **Belgium, Ireland, Portugal, Czechia and Poland outside Pepper PL** are likely also underserved at city level **[est, not verified traffic]**.

## 3. Where nobody combines supermarket promos + local events + student deals

No player found in any European country bundles all three by city. Today the pieces sit in silos: flyers (Bonial, Profital, ShopFully), deals (Pepper, Preispirat), student offers (UNiDAYS, Pion, ISIC), campus apps (UniNow, Studo), anti-waste (TGTG). The clearest whitespace:
1. **Swiss university towns** (St. Gallen → Zürich, Bern, Basel, Lausanne/EPFL, Geneva, Fribourg), with FR/DE content. That covers about 270k students in Switzerland **[mem]**.
2. **Mid-size DACH university towns** (Innsbruck, Graz, Konstanz, Freiburg, Heidelberg), where national apps exist but no local curator does.
3. **Erasmus-heavy cities** in BE, PT and CZ (Leuven, Ghent, Lisbon, Porto, Prague, Brno).

Suggested path: CH first, then AT/South-DE towns (same language, same flyer data model), then Erasmus cities in English.

## 4. Biggest threats

- **Incumbents adding a "student" or "nearby" tab.** Profital/Bring! (Swiss Post funding and retailer contracts), UNiDAYS/Pion (verification and brand network), and Bonial if it enters CH.
- **Data access.** Flyer feeds are commercial: Bonial and Profital are paid by retailers, while scraping Migros/Coop is legally and technically fragile. Rappn is already building this layer.
- **Affiliate fragility.** The Honey scandal, Chrome policy changes and Booking.com's 2024 exit show commissions can disappear overnight. Small CH affiliate volume caps revenue.
- **Seasonality and churn.** Students graduate yearly, and the semester calendar drives usage.
- **Cold start.** Community deal sites need density, and Preispirat already holds the Swiss enthusiasts.

## 5. Defensible differentiation

1. **City and campus density over breadth.** Run campus ambassadors, partner with student associations (e.g. SHSG at HSG), and capture the semester-start moment. National players cannot copy this cheaply.
2. **Neutral scoring.** Flyer apps are retailer-paid. A transparent score (per-unit price vs. history, using Toppreise-type data) is a trust moat.
3. **Local merchants and events inventory** (bars, gyms, cinemas, uni events) sold to **local sponsors**. UNiDAYS and Pepper do not serve this long tail.
4. **Instant alerts** (premium) through WhatsApp/Telegram/push, filtered by campus and budget. Preispirat shows the channel works.
5. **Partner rather than fight** with TGTG, poinz, DayDeal/QoQa, Comparis and Beans iD as feeds, links or verification.
6. **Bilingual DE/FR from day one**, which makes CH-wide coverage and the FR/AT expansions cheap.

Data caveats: no recent per-site Similarweb numbers could be pulled for mydealz, Dealabs, Profital, Preispirat or UNiDAYS. Verify them with Similarweb before any investor-facing use.
